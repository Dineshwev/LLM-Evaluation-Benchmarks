## Key Citation-Quality Patterns

### Pattern 1: Source Support vs. Claim Specificity
When claims become more specific (e.g., "biometric categorization by sensitive attributes" vs. just "biometric identification" or "manipulate human behaviour" vs. "subliminal and deceptive techniques"), the citation must provide equally specific evidence. General citations to broad sections may not suffice for narrow claims.

### Pattern 2: Fact Accuracy ≠ Citation Sufficiency
Claims can be factually accurate per the Act while still having incomplete citations. Claims 2, 4, and 7 demonstrate this: the underlying idea is valid, but the exact wording or classification distinction in the response outpaces the specific evidence cited.

### Pattern 3: Implicit Context
Claims presented between cited sections (like Claim 9) may lack explicit citations even when factually accurate. Citation placement standards require citations for each independently verifiable claim.

### Pattern 4: Source Choice Matters
Some claims are better supported by specific sources. Claim 4 (biometric categorization) would be better supported by SureCloud or GDPR Advisor, which explicitly address sensitive-attribute inference, rather than a broad Wikipedia reference to general biometric identification. The same principle applies to claim wording that adds legal-specific distinctions not directly stated in the summary source.
